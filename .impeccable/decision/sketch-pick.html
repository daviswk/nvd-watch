Highlights · What we found, and who should act

Why this matters

Since April 15, 2026, NIST fully enriches only CVEs in CISA's KEV catalog, federal software, or EO 14028 critical software. Everything else waits.

Without a CPE record, vulnerability scanners cannot tell an organization that it is affected. State and local governments, hospitals, and small businesses rely on this free data.

NIST asked the public (RFI 7(e)) what metrics should track the NVD's success. This page is a working answer.

Data: NVD API 2.0, CISA KEV, FIRST EPSS. Pulled Nov 7, 2026, 19:21 UTC. 17,729 records.

What we found

22.6%of CVEs published in the last 30 days carry CPE. KEV-listed CVEs: 94%. Not Scheduled CVEs: 0%.
  • KEV is the gate. For most software, CPE arrives only after a KEV listing, and the median CVE-to-KEV lag has risen from 6 to 14 days since 2023.
  • The fix costs less than what NIST already spends re-scoring. A published re-check rule: roughly $270K–$510K a year against an OIG-identified $400K of redirectable effort.

What we recommend

NISTPublish a re-check rule for Not Scheduled CVEs; publish five quarterly metrics with frozen definitions.
CISARequire structured product data in CNA records; publish KEV listing lag.
CongressAttach the metrics to the pending GAO review and NDAA CVE language, not new spending.
