Why this matters

New CVEs in the NVD rose from 5,143 in January 2026 to 17,481 in October (our count). Google's threat intelligence team sees early signs that AI is speeding up vulnerability discovery, though it ties much of the rise to other causes, such as bulk Linux kernel CVEs.

Since April 15, 2026, NIST fully enriches only CVEs that are in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, in federal software, or in software deemed critical under Executive Order 14028. Everything else waits, most of it under the status "Not Scheduled."

Enrichment is what attaches a CPE record to a CVE. Without it, vulnerability scanners cannot tell an organization that it is affected. State and local governments, hospitals, and small businesses depend on this free data because they cannot buy a commercial substitute.

NIST asked the public what metrics should track the NVD's success (RFI question 7(e), August 2026). This page is a working answer, rebuilt weekly from the NVD's own API.

What we recommend

NISTPublish a re-check rule that moves CVEs without CPE into the KEV queue when attack signals rise, log every re-queue, report five quarterly metrics with frozen definitions, and publish each CVE's priority tier (or quarterly counts by tier, where per-CVE tags would expose agency software).
CISAAs CVE Program sponsor, phase in structured product data in CNA records, largest vendor CNAs first; as KEV owner, publish listing lag and speed review when credible third-party exploitation reports arrive.

How this differs from the figures in circulation

Published coverage estimates range from about 50% (Volerion, April to June 2026) to 8.4% (Cyber Strategy Institute, Q3 2026) because each uses a different definition and window. Ours counts the share of non-rejected CVEs published in the last 30 days whose NVD record has a CPE configuration, and the definition does not change.

Coverage also rises as CVEs age: of the CVEs published April 15 to June 15, 2026, 57% had CPE by November 7. A 30-day window shows what defenders have while a vulnerability is new, which is when they need it.

2The gate

For software outside NIST's federal and critical-software priorities, CPE arrives only after CISA lists the CVE as exploited. That listing has slowed since 2023, and it comes after attacks have begun.

Did defenders have CPE when they needed it? We define "when they needed it" as before CISA confirmed exploitation, and score two things separately. Readiness: of the {{ke_l_pub}} CVEs added to KEV in this window that were already public (and not enriched before the April 2026 triage), {{ke_l_ready}} had CPE before the listing. Where EPSS fits

EPSS, FIRST's daily exploitation-probability score, led KEV in only 19% of cases, but by about 201 days when it did (Barracuda). It is not an intake screen for brand-new CVEs: in this window only {{epss_ge_010}} CVEs without CPE and outside KEV scored 0.10 or higher ({{epss_ge_005}} at 0.05; {{epss_ge_001}} at 0.01), and {{epss_missing}} had no score yet. It is a re-check trigger for the stock of CVEs that were set aside.

The kind of CVE the rule would catch: CVE-2026-59774, published October 14, 2026, lets an unauthenticated attacker read server files through Gitea's Org-mode renderer and, in some configurations, chain that into running commands on the server (CNA description). Gitea is self-hosted code-hosting software. NIST does not publish which tier it assigned, but the record is Not Scheduled, with no CPE, although the CNA names the product and affected versions in free text. On November 7 its EPSS score was 0.28. It is not in KEV; we have not checked other exploitation sources. Under the rule it would be re-queued now; today nothing brings it back.

The score matures after disclosure. CVE Floodline (RogoLabs) re-scores each day's CVEs against the dated EPSS archive: of {{fl_n}} CVEs old enough to have a day-30 score, {{fl_cross10_day0}} had crossed 10% on their disclosure day, {{fl_cross10_day7}} by day 7, and {{fl_cross10_day30}} by day 30 (data as of {{fl_generated}}). A low day-one score is provisional, not safe, which is why the trigger has to keep re-checking.

3The fix and its cost

A published re-check rule: when the attack signal on a CVE without CPE crosses a threshold, move it into the queue NIST already runs for KEV entries and log the move. No new money; well under one percent of existing capacity.

The rule

Move any CVE without CPE (Not Scheduled, Awaiting Enrichment, or still Received) into the KEV queue, under NIST's existing one-business-day goal, when the maximum of its EPSS score, its KEV status, and its LEV estimate (NIST's own composite, CSWP 41) crosses a published threshold, when CISA's SSVC decision marks exploitation as proof-of-concept or active, or when credible third-party exploitation evidence arrives. Publish the threshold. Publish every re-queue and its time to enrichment. Moving a CVE from Not Scheduled to the back of the Awaiting queue would not be enough: {{awaiting_pct}} of new CVEs already wait there with no CPE.

NIST already designed the composite; FedRAMP's 2026 rules already use KEV. The rule adds nothing that does not exist, except the commitment to apply it and to say so.

4Who should act

Three stakeholders, one rule, five numbers. The metrics below answer NIST's RFI question 7(e) and are defined so that they cannot drift.

Recommendations

NISTAdopt and publish the re-check rule and its threshold. Log every re-queue. Publish the five metrics quarterly with the definitions below, and a change log whenever a status category changes. Publish each CVE's priority tier (KEV, federal software, EO 14028 critical software, or none), so outsiders can check the triage itself. If tagging a CVE as federal software would reveal what agencies run, publish quarterly counts by tier instead, which is enough to audit metrics 1 and 2.
CISAAs CVE Program sponsor, phase in a requirement for affected-product data in a structured, machine-matchable form in CNA records, starting with the largest vendor CNAs and building on the Supplier ADP pilot, so that CPE assignment is a lookup, not research. Many CNAs are small or volunteer-run, and a sudden mandate could push them to publish fewer CVEs. As KEV owner, publish listing lag by month and commit to a review clock when credible third-party exploitation reports arrive.
CongressThrough the Appropriations, Science, and Homeland Security committees: ask GAO to include these metrics in the CVE program review that House Homeland Security and Science ranking members requested in June 2025, and write them into any bill that codifies the CVE program (neither chamber's FY2027 NDAA text does). Treat the rule as a use of existing capacity (under 1% of peak output), not a new line.

One limit, stated plainly

Some threats never reach a CVE record defenders can match. The August 2026 joint advisory on attacks against Siemens S7 PLCs listed no CVE IDs at all, pointing defenders to the vendor's own advisories. No enrichment rule helps there. This page measures the system that exists.

Five quarterly metrics

  1. Share of new CVEs with CPEAny source, and NIST-sourced, by observable tier: KEV status and NVD status. NIST does not publish which CVEs it placed in the federal or EO 14028 tiers, so no outsider can report by those tiers until it does. Denominator: CVEs published in the quarter, Rejected excluded.
  2. Time to enrichmentMedian and 90th percentile days from CVE publication to first CPE, by the same observable tiers. Measured from the NVD change history, not last-modified.
  3. KEV enrichment complianceShare of KEV-listed CVEs enriched within NIST's one-business-day goal, timed from the NVD change history. This window: {{ke_l_1bd}} of {{ke_l_n}} CVEs added to KEV.
  4. Re-queue counts and outcomesCVEs re-queued under the rule per month, by trigger, and their time to enrichment after re-queue.
  5. Status definition change logEvery change to an NVD status category, its date, and the mapping from old to new. The categories changed on April 15, 2026; the current mapping is in the definitions below.

Definitions

Frozen on {{pulled_date}}. Changes are recorded here, never made silently.

CVE
A published vulnerability record in the CVE List, run by the CVE Program and sponsored by CISA. Records are written by CVE Numbering Authorities (CNAs).
CPE
Common Platform Enumeration, NIST's standardized product identifier. On the page, "with CPE" means the NVD record carries a configurations block. Scanners match on it.
Enrichment
NIST's process of adding CPE, CWE, reference tags, and, where needed, a CVSS score to a CVE record.
NVD statuses
Display label (API value): Received (Received); Awaiting Enrichment (Awaiting Analysis); Undergoing Enrichment (Undergoing Analysis); Enriched (Analyzed); Modified After Enrichment (Modified); Not Scheduled (Deferred); Rejected (Rejected). From nvd.nist.gov/vuln/vulnerability-status, updated April 14, 2026.
Not Scheduled
NIST's label for a CVE "not currently scheduled for NVD enrichment efforts," because it is out of scope or not prioritized. Requests to schedule it may be made.
KEV
CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. A listing is one of NIST's enrichment triggers, with a one-business-day enrichment goal.
EPSS
FIRST's Exploit Prediction Scoring System: a daily probability (0 to 1) that a CVE is exploited in the next 30 days.
LEV
Likely Exploited Vulnerabilities, NIST CSWP 41 (May 2025): a composite that takes the maximum of EPSS, KEV, and a historical likelihood estimate.
SSVC
CISA's Stakeholder-Specific Vulnerability Categorization. Its exploitation field is none, proof-of-concept, or active.
Window
CVEs with an NVD publication date in the 30 days before the pull. Rejected records are excluded from every share.
