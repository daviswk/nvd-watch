# AI-Driven Vulnerability Discovery, Disclosure Volume, Time-to-Exploit, and Implications for Public Vulnerability Databases (2025 – Nov 6, 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-06. Primary sources were preferred and fetched directly where possible. Vendor self-interest is flagged inline as **[VENDOR]**. Claims I could not verify are flagged as **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## Q1. Verify the Mandiant time-to-exploit (TTE) figures from the original reports, with any 2024/2025/2026 updates

### Takeaway
The RAND commentary's "63 days (2018–19) → 5 days (2023)" is accurate to Mandiant's original Oct 2024 report, but the 5-day figure excludes outliers (about 47 days if they are included). The newest Mandiant figure (M-Trends 2026, March 2026) is a mean TTE of **–7 days**, meaning exploitation on average comes before the patch. I found no standalone Mandiant TTE report covering 2024.

### Cited Findings
- **Original TTE report:** Mandiant (Casey Charrier and Robert Weiner), "How Low Can You Go? An Analysis of 2023 Time-to-Exploit Trends," published **Oct 15, 2024**. Average TTE was **63 days in 2018–2019, 44 days in 2020 to early 2021, 32 days in 2021–2022, and 5 days in 2023** — [Google Cloud / Mandiant](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/time-to-exploit-trends-2023)
  - Methodology caveats from the same report: TTE is defined as "the average time taken to exploit a vulnerability before or after a patch is released." The sample was 138 vulnerabilities disclosed in 2023 and exploited in the wild: 97 zero-days (~70%) and 41 n-days (~30%). Fifteen outliers (13 zero-days, 2 n-days) were excluded using a standard-deviation method. **Including the outliers, the 2023 average is about 47 days.** Vague exploitation dates were assigned the latest plausible date, and the authors call the figures conservative — [same source](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/time-to-exploit-trends-2023)
  - The zero-day share rose: the n-day:zero-day ratio was 39:61 (2020–21), 38:62 (2021–22), and 30:70 (2023). Because zero-days count as exploited before a patch, a rising zero-day share mechanically pulls the average TTE down. That is a measurement artifact the student should note — [same source](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/time-to-exploit-trends-2023)
- **Latest update:** Mandiant M-Trends 2026 (blog by Jurgen Kutscher, published **Mar 23, 2026**) says: "the mean time to exploit vulnerabilities dropped to an estimated **-7 days**, meaning exploitation is routinely occurring before a patch is even released." Exploits were the top initial infection vector for the sixth straight year (**32% of intrusions**). Global median dwell time **rose** from 11 to 14 days — [Google Cloud / M-Trends 2026](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026/); executive edition PDF: [services.google.com](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/m-trends-2026-executive-edition-en.pdf)
  - M-Trends 2026 explicitly plays down AI as the cause of 2025 breaches: "we do not consider 2025 to be the year where breaches were the direct result of AI." It attributes most intrusions to "fundamental human and systemic failures." The blog does **not** say AI caused the drop in TTE — [same source](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026/)
- Mandiant's July 16, 2026 "Blueprint for AI-Assisted Vulnerability Management" repeats the –7 day mean from M-Trends 2026 — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/ai-assisted-vulnerability-management)
- GTIG's Sep 30, 2026 "AI Era" report gives **no aggregate TTE**. Its only timing example is BeyondTrust CVE-2026-1731: one threat cluster was exploiting it within 4 days of disclosure, and five more clusters within 7 days — [Google Cloud / GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/vulnerability-discovery-and-exploitation-trends-in-the-ai-era)
- A non-Mandiant measure, reported second-hand: Rapid7 found that exploited high/critical vulnerabilities rose 105% in 2025 (71 → 146), and that the **median** time from publication to exploitation fell from 8.5 to 5.0 days. The CSA whitepaper is the source here; I did not check Rapid7's original — [CSA whitepaper, May 4, 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-whitepaper-nvd-infrastructure-crisis-ai-vulnerability-di/)
- A "mean exploit time 2.1 days" claim circulating online cites only Reddit and names no Mandiant report. **Do not use it** — [aiweekly.co](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/mandiant-data-mean-exploit-time-drops-to-21-days)

### Inferences
- The TTE series is a **mean** that is sensitive to outliers and to the zero-day share. A move from +5 to –7 days mostly reflects more exploitation before a patch exists (zero-days), not necessarily faster weaponization of n-days. That makes it weak evidence on its own that AI discovery is accelerating exploitation. It is strong evidence that the defensive patch window has closed.
- Mandiant itself declined to credit AI for 2025 breaches. The student should not present the TTE collapse as AI-caused without that caveat. Most of the TTE decline (63 → 5 days) happened in 2018–2023, before capable AI vulnerability tools existed.

### Gaps
- I could not find a standalone Mandiant/GTIG TTE report covering **2024** (the 2023 report is dated Oct 2024). M-Trends 2026's –7 day figure presumably covers 2025 incidents, but the blog does not state the sample or year. The full M-Trends 2026 PDF should be checked for methodology and n.
- I did not verify the RAND "Tipping the Cyber Balance" commentary text or RAND RR-A3892-2 directly. Both were out of scope as already in hand.

## Q2. Other measurements of AI-driven discovery volume and quality; share of new CVEs that are AI-found; noise or signal?

### Takeaway
By the second half of 2026, AI-found vulnerabilities are numerous and mostly **real**. The 2025 "slop" problem (fabricated reports) has largely given way to "high volume, high quality," in curl's maintainer's words. No source can say what share of CVEs are AI-found, because CVE records carry no AI-attribution field. Monthly CVE volume roughly doubled in 2026, while observed exploitation stayed a tiny fraction (0.23%). So the flood is real signal about bugs but mostly not signal about attacker activity.

### Cited Findings
**Aggregate volume**
- GTIG (Grunewald, Mazumdar, Vanderlee; published **Sep 30, 2026**) counted monthly disclosed vulnerabilities rising "from **5,045** in January 2026 to 10,477 in July... to **10,740** in August 2026." Only "**0.23%** of all disclosed vulnerabilities in 2026 (roughly 1 in 431) were ever observed in active exploitation." Exploited vulnerabilities averaged 10.5/month in 2025 and 18/month in Jan–Aug 2026. Over Jan–Aug 2026, 141 vulnerabilities were disclosed and exploited, more than the 127 for all of 2025 — [GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/vulnerability-discovery-and-exploitation-trends-in-the-ai-era) **[VENDOR: Google sells AI security products]**
  - On AI attribution, GTIG says "Current public data significantly undercount vulnerabilities discovered by AI." CVE repositories "do not yet feature uniform metadata tags for AI attribution," and cloud providers "routinely remediate AI-surfaced vulnerabilities directly in production without requesting formal CVE IDs." It gives **no overall AI share** — [GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/vulnerability-discovery-and-exploitation-trends-in-the-ai-era)
  - On AI-found quality, GTIG says "Exactly 50% of all AI-discovered vulnerabilities result in Remote Code Execution (RCE), compared to just 26%" ecosystem-wide. On the noise side, "Linux Kernel" CVEs alone were about 5,000 from Jan–Aug 2026 "with zero observed exploited in-the-wild zero-days." GTIG adds that "raw disclosure volume throughout 2026 can be misleading without threat intelligence context" — [GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/vulnerability-discovery-and-exploitation-trends-in-the-ai-era)
  - Arithmetic caveats: "doubled" is +113% (Jan → Aug). Exploitation "nearly doubled" is actually +71%. Some comparisons set a full year (2025) against eight months (2026). Figure 7's indexing baseline is unclear.
- FIRST "2026 Vulnerability Forecast Update: Navigating the AI Epoch" (Jerry Gamblin and Eireann Leverett, **Jun 15, 2026**): cumulative CVEs were running **+46.3%** above the original forecast, an excess of 6,420. The updated median is **~66K** CVEs for 2026 in the body text (page metadata says ~68K). FIRST credits Anthropic's Glasswing with a 164% spike in Q1 Mozilla disclosures. It also names **non-AI drivers**: GitHub Security Advisory volume up 449% YoY, and VulnCheck up 3,119% YoY as CNA of Last Resort — [FIRST](https://www.first.org/blog/20260615-vulnerability-forecast-update)
- NIST: CVE submissions rose **263%** from 2020 to 2025. Q1 2026 ran nearly one-third above Q1 2025. NIST enriched ~42,000 CVEs in 2025, 45% more than any prior year. NIST's notice does **not** mention AI as a cause — [NIST, Apr 15, 2026](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/04/nist-updates-nvd-operations-address-record-cve-growth)
- 48,185 CVEs were published in 2025. CSA cites NIST/NVD for this; I did not check it against cve.org — [CSA whitepaper](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-whitepaper-nvd-infrastructure-crisis-ai-vulnerability-di/)

**Specific AI discovery programs**
- **Anthropic Project Glasswing / Claude Mythos Preview** (initial update, **May 22, 2026**). About 50 partners found ">10,000 high- or critical-severity vulnerabilities." Across 1,000+ open-source projects, Mythos found 23,019 vulnerabilities, an estimated 6,202 of them high/critical. Of 1,752 findings assessed by six independent firms or Anthropic, **1,587 were valid (90.6% true positive)** and 1,094 were confirmed high/critical. About 530 high/critical bugs had been disclosed, but only **75 were patched and 65 had public advisories**. Only one CVE is named (CVE-2026-5194, wolfSSL). Mozilla fixed 271 vulnerabilities in Firefox 150, and Palo Alto Networks' latest release had more than 5x its usual patch count — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/glasswing-initial-update) **[VENDOR]**
  - Discrepancy: Security Affairs reported "1,726 confirmed," which does not match Anthropic's primary figures (1,587 valid of 1,752 assessed). Use the primary — [Security Affairs, May 24, 2026](https://securityaffairs.com/192576/ai/anthropics-glasswing-10000-vulnerabilities-found-in-one-month-and-the-patching-problem-has-never-been-more-obvious.html)
  - Independent check: curl's maintainer reported that Mythos's first scan of curl found **one low-severity issue** — [Stenberg, "The pressure," May 26, 2026](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/05/26/the-pressure/); see also [Stenberg, "Mythos finds a curl vulnerability," May 11, 2026](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/05/11/mythos-finds-a-curl-vulnerability/)
- **Google Big Sleep**: 20 vulnerabilities reported in open-source projects including FFmpeg-class multimedia frameworks, ImageMagick-class converters, and Redis. All were rated P2, and Google said they were found and reproduced "without human intervention" (reported **Aug 5, 2025**) — [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/google-publishes-20-new-vulnerabilities-found-by-its-big-sleep-ai-619298). Later reports cover 5 Safari/WebKit vulnerabilities (Nov 2025) — [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/11/googles-ai-big-sleep-finds-5-new.html) **[VENDOR]**
- **DARPA AIxCC Final** (results announced **Aug 8, 2025**). Teams found **54 of 63 synthetic vulns (86%)** and patched **43 (68%)**, up from 37% and 25% at the semifinals. They also found **18 real (non-synthetic) vulnerabilities** (6 in C, 12 in Java) and supplied 11 patches. Average cost was about **$152 per task** across 54M+ lines of code. Winners were Team Atlanta, Trail of Bits, and Theori, and all seven finalist systems are being open-sourced — [DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/aixcc-results)
- **XBOW on HackerOne** (blog, **Jun 24, 2025**): about 1,060 submissions. Of these, 130 were resolved, 303 triaged, 208 duplicate, 209 informative, 36 N/A, and 125 pending. Program owners rated 54 critical, 242 high, 524 medium, and 65 low over 90 days. XBOW uses automated "validators" (e.g., a headless browser to confirm XSS) to limit false positives — [XBOW](https://xbow.com/blog/top-1-how-xbow-did-it/) **[VENDOR: XBOW is a commercial startup; numbers are self-reported]**
  - Inference from these numbers: only about 12% were resolved, and about 40% were duplicate or informative. That is a meaningful triage burden on program owners even with validators.
- **OpenAI**: I could **not verify** the claim of "GPT-6 Cyber, Nov 5, 2026, 588 previously unknown Linux kernel bugs." Searches found GPT-5.4-Cyber (cited by FIRST), GPT-5.5-Cyber with a "Patch the Planet" open-source initiative, GPT-5.6-Cyber (Aug 2026; [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/openai-launches-gpt-56-cyber-with.html)), and Fortune/Reuters reports (Sep 24, 2026) that OpenAI was **preparing** a GPT-6 Cyber model. A tech-insider article says OpenAI had published no confirmation, model card, or count as of its writing, and no source I found mentions "588" — [tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/openai-gpt-6-cyber-4th-security-model-2026/). **[UNVERIFIED]** The announcement may be too recent (Nov 5) for search indexes, so the student should cite OpenAI's primary page directly.
- **Anthropic formal verification** (announced **Oct 22, 2026**, with Theorem). Claude wrote Rocq proofs that 239 OSS-Fuzz entry points across 123 open-source libraries do not crash under stated conditions (60 entry points in 25 libraries are public). "Hundreds of bugs" were found along the way, not all of them new — [Anthropic primary](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification), via [Xenospectrum summary, Oct 24, 2026](https://xenospectrum.com/en/claude-formal-verification-oss-proofs/) **[VENDOR]**

**Linux kernel CNA volume**
- The Linux kernel was the **#1 CVE vendor in H1 2026, with 2,308 CVEs**. Greg Kroah-Hartman said: "I gotta change my talk where I say 'we are #2' as that's not the case by far anymore." - GTIG counted about 5,000 "Linux Kernel" CVEs from Jan–Aug 2026 with zero exploited in-the-wild zero-days. GTIG attributes this to "automated CNA assignment policies," i.e., a CNA policy effect, not an AI effect — [GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/vulnerability-discovery-and-exploitation-trends-in-the-ai-era)

**curl: from "slop" to signal**
- Jul 2025: "Death by a thousand slops" — [Stenberg](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2025/07/14/death-by-a-thousand-slops/). In Jan 2026, curl ended its paid bug bounty effective end of January 2026 to "remove the incentive for people to submit crap and non-well researched reports to us. AI generated or not" — [The Register, Jan 21, 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/21/curl_ends_bug_bounty/)
- Apr 22, 2026, "High-Quality Chaos": "Almost every security report now uses AI to various degrees." Report frequency is "higher than ever," about double 2025. The confirmed-vulnerability rate is back to about **15–16%** (the 2024 pre-AI level). Curl may publish "closer to 50" CVEs in 2026, and Stenberg cites similar trends at Apache httpd, BIND, Django, Firefox, the Linux kernel, and wolfSSL (informal poll) — [Stenberg](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/04/22/high-quality-chaos/)
- Oct 22, 2026, "ignore the slop": "Since spring 2026 the slop reports are practically gone." The challenge is now "high volume, high quality." He adds: "Ignoring real vulnerabilities because they were found by AI does not make users safe and secure" — [Stenberg](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/10/22/ignore-the-slop/)

### Inferences
- The "noise vs. signal" question splits in two. (a) **Report-level noise** (fabricated reports) was the 2025 problem and had largely receded by spring 2026 as models improved. (b) **Risk-level noise** remains: most newly disclosed, often AI-found CVEs are real bugs that are rarely exploited (0.23%). This supports a **triage** argument, not a "flood of junk" argument.
- Part of the CVE surge is **structural, not AI-caused**: kernel CNA policy, VulnCheck as CNA of Last Resort (+3,119%), and GHSA growth (+449%). The student should present AI as one of several drivers, and the hardest to quantify.
- The 2026 jump (5K → 10.7K/month) coincides with Glasswing and Mythos (April–May 2026) and with maintainers' reports of quality changing around March 2026. The timing is suggestive, but no source isolates the AI-caused share.

### Gaps
- No source gives the **share of 2026 CVEs that were AI-discovered**. GTIG explicitly says it cannot be measured from public data.
- Glasswing's CVE count is unknown; Anthropic names only one CVE. See also [Let's Data Science, "Glasswing Leaves CVE Count Uncertain"](https://letsdatascience.com/news/anthropics-glasswing-leaves-cve-count-uncertain-c9d7c1ac), which I did not fetch.

- Kernel CVE totals for 2024 and 2025 were not retrieved.

## Q3. Maintainer and disclosure-process strain; policy proposals

### Takeaway
There is strong first-hand evidence of strain from the most-cited open-source projects (curl, the Linux kernel) and from Anthropic's own disclosure data: patch rates lag far behind findings. Responses so far are **project-level rule changes** (ending bounties, kernel AI-report rules, slower disclosure on request). They are not systemic policy. The most developed systemic proposals come from the Cloud Security Alliance and are mostly about governance (human accountability, platform rules), not throttling.

### Cited Findings
- **Linux kernel**: Linus Torvalds (LKML, around **May 17, 2026**, with 7.1-rc4) called the private security list "almost entirely unmanageable" and said "AI detected bugs are pretty much by definition not secret." Willy Tarreau reported that volume went from about 2–3 reports/week two years earlier to **5–10 per day**, with many duplicates as people run the same tools on the same code — [Tom's Hardware, May 18, 2026](https://tomshardware.com/software/linux/linus-torvalds-says-ai-bug-reports-have-made-the-linux-security-mailing-list-almost-entirely-unmanageable)
  - The policy response: new kernel documentation (merged for Linux 7.1) says AI-found bugs should go **publicly** to maintainers rather than the private security list. Reports must be brief plain text with a verified reproducer. AI agents may not use "Signed-off-by"; an "Assisted-by" tag is required — [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Linux-7.1-Kernel-Docs-AI-Bugs); patch thread: [LKML](https://lkml.iu.edu/2605.0/01769.html)
- **curl**: security reports are 4–5x the 2024 rate and about 2x 2025, averaging more than one per day. Twelve confirmed vulnerabilities were awaiting CVEs mid-cycle (a project record), and 30 CVEs were published before mid-2026. Stenberg spends almost all of his ~50-hour weeks on the queue: "a never-before seen or experienced pressure on the curl project"; "There's a tsunami coming over us and all we can do is swim, there are no life boats for us." He asks dependent companies to fund developers — [Stenberg, May 26, 2026](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/05/26/the-pressure/). Earlier he wrote: "This avalanche is going to make maintainer overload even worse" — [Stenberg, Apr 22, 2026](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/04/22/high-quality-chaos/)
- **Anthropic's own data on the remediation gap**: of about 530 disclosed high/critical bugs, only 75 were patched. 827 more confirmed vulnerabilities were queued for disclosure. Several maintainers are "severely capacity constrained," and some **asked Anthropic to slow its disclosure rate**, which it says it is doing at a "relatively slow pace" within a 90-day window. Maintainers also face "a deluge of low-quality, AI-generated bug reports" from others — [Anthropic, May 22, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/glasswing-initial-update)
- **FIRST**: "The foundational constraint of the Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure (CVD) ecosystem is human capacity." FIRST notes that CVE processing delays may reflect staffing, not a safer internet — [FIRST, Jun 15, 2026](https://www.first.org/blog/20260615-vulnerability-forecast-update)
- **Vendors**: Oracle moved to a monthly critical update cycle. Microsoft has said patch volume will keep trending higher — [Security Affairs, May 24, 2026](https://securityaffairs.com/192576/ai/anthropics-glasswing-10000-vulnerabilities-found-in-one-month-and-the-patching-problem-has-never-been-more-obvious.html) (secondary).
- **Policy proposals (CSA, "Reforming Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure for the Autonomous Bug Hunter Era," Jun 7, 2026)**:
  - A designated human operator should be the accountable disclosing party for AI findings.
  - Bounty platforms should revise terms to require human review before submission, require disclosure of AI tools, and define when automated batch submissions are allowed. HackerOne's human-review policy is the baseline.
  - Prioritize findings by EPSS/KEV before 90-day clocks start.
  - Consider Project Zero–style "Reporting Transparency."
  - CISA JCDC and the NIST Cyber AI Profile should formalize CVD standards for autonomous agents.
  - CSA proposes **no numeric throttling** — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-vulnerability-disclosure-policy-reform) **[Advocacy org]**
  - CSA also notes vendor intake spikes YoY of Chrome +563%, VMware +181%, Apache +170%, and Mozilla +157%, plus a "silent bounty" problem in which valid findings are paid but never receive a CVE — [same](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-vulnerability-disclosure-policy-reform)
- **Curl's response**: it ended the paid bounty (end of Jan 2026), moved intake back to HackerOne unpaid (Mar 2026), and per a secondary source paused intake for five weeks (Jul 1 – Aug 3, 2026) **[UNVERIFIED primary]** — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/21/curl_ends_bug_bounty/); [Pinggy](https://pinggy.io/amp/blog/curl_ai_slop_summer_of_bliss/)

### Inferences
- The binding constraint has moved from **finding** to **fixing and recording**. For Glasswing, roughly 14% of disclosed high/critical bugs were patched at the first update. curl's CVE pipeline is backlogged within the project before anything reaches the CVE/NVD layer. That is the same capacity mismatch the NVD faces, one level upstream.
- "Disclosure throttling" exists today only informally, through maintainer requests to individual finders. No platform-level or government-level rate limit was found.
- The kernel rule that AI-found bugs are effectively public cuts against traditional embargoed CVD. That raises questions for CNAs about whether such bugs get CVEs and when.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence that a **CNA** (as distinct from a maintainer) formally reported inability to keep up, or no quantified CNA backlog data.
- I found no HackerOne or Bugcrowd primary policy text on AI submissions in 2026. CSA references HackerOne's human-review rule second-hand.

## Q4. Counter-evidence: AI improving defense/enrichment, flat exploitation

### Takeaway
The strongest counter-evidence is that **actionable exploitability is flat** (FIRST) and that only 0.23% of 2026 CVEs were seen exploited (GTIG). Disclosure volume overstates operational risk. Claims that AI will **fix enrichment** remain proposals without measured results. NIST mentions only future "automated systems," with no AI.

### Cited Findings
- FIRST: total disclosures show a "Massive Increase," but **actionable exploitability (CISA KEV or EPSS >10%) is flat/stable**. The patching burden for live systems should stay steady through 2026, and the task is separating signal from noise. FIRST advises budgeting by software growth, not CVE growth — [FIRST, Jun 15, 2026](https://www.first.org/blog/20260615-vulnerability-forecast-update)
  - Partial tension: GTIG shows absolute exploited counts **rising** (10.5 → 18/month; zero-days 8 → 11/month, 22 in August 2026) even though the exploited **share** is tiny — [GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/vulnerability-discovery-and-exploitation-trends-in-the-ai-era). Flat exploitability as a ratio (FIRST) and rising exploited counts in absolute terms (GTIG) can both hold. The student should say which is meant.
- GTIG suggests AI could eventually **reduce** disclosure growth: "If pre-release AI code review becomes standard best practice, the rate of growth in public vulnerability disclosures could eventually slow." It also notes many AI-found cloud bugs are fixed silently without CVEs — [GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/vulnerability-discovery-and-exploitation-trends-in-the-ai-era)
- Defensive remediation at scale: Anthropic says Claude Security patched over 2,100 vulnerabilities in three weeks (Claude Opus 4.7) — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/glasswing-initial-update) **[VENDOR, self-reported]**. AIxCC systems auto-patched 68% of synthetic vulns at about $152/task — [DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/aixcc-results). Anthropic/Theorem's formal verification work proves the absence of crash classes in fuzzed entry points — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification) **[VENDOR]**
- Curl's Willy Tarreau (HAProxy) argues the upside: fixing old, low-importance bugs now may reduce future emergency releases and CVEs — [Stenberg comments, Apr 22, 2026](https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/04/22/high-quality-chaos/)
- **AI-assisted enrichment is untested**:
  - CSA's "AI-Assisted NVD Enrichment" whitepaper (Apr 25, 2026) proposes a 1,000-CVE, ~6-month pilot with an $85K funding request. Its targets are ≥85% CPE accuracy and ≥90% CVSS-validation accuracy, and it explicitly does **not** claim AI matches expert enrichment. It reports **no measured results** and says human review remains necessary — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/vulnerability-management-ai-assisted-nvd-enrichment-v3/)
  - Mandiant's AI vulnerability-management blueprint (Jul 16, 2026) reports **no empirical results**. It cautions that LLMs hallucinate success, miss logic bugs, and "do not guarantee exhaustive coverage" — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/ai-assisted-vulnerability-management)
  - NIST's Apr 2026 notice mentions only developing "automated systems and workflow enhancements required for long-term sustainability," not AI — [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/04/nist-updates-nvd-operations-address-record-cve-growth)
- **Value of NVD's own scoring is questionable**: the Commerce OIG (report **OIG-26-020-I**, ~late May 2026) found that NIST severity scores matched independent assessors only **12%** of the time. It found that 80% of submissions already carry CNA scores, and it recommended **reducing** NIST severity scoring as of "negligible value" (est. ~$800K saved over two years). The backlog grew from ~13,000 (Feb 2024) to >27,000 (end of 2025). NIST never processed more than 5,000/month against a 6,200/month target, and NIST and CISA duplicated work in at least 21,000 instances — [The Record, Jun 1, 2026](https://therecord.media/nist-mistakes-vulnerability-database-inspector-general); [OIG abstract PDF](https://www.oig.doc.gov/wp-content/OIGPublications/OIG-26-020-I_Abstract-SECURED.pdf)
  - This cuts **both ways** for the student's thesis. NVD's narrowing may cost less than feared, because CNA scores already exist and NVD scores were often discordant. But NVD's pre-AI capacity was already inadequate, so the April 2026 decision is not purely an AI response.
- VulnCheck found that NVD provided CPE for 41.35% of vulnerabilities versus 76.95% from VulnCheck's own service, and that NVD and CNA CVSS disagreed in more than half of dual-scored cases. The CSA whitepaper is the source here — [CSA whitepaper](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-whitepaper-nvd-infrastructure-crisis-ai-vulnerability-di/) **[VENDOR: VulnCheck sells enrichment]**

### Inferences
- The best counter-argument to "AI flood breaks NVD" runs as follows. Exploitation-relevant CVEs (KEV, high EPSS) are flat or growing modestly. NIST's April 2026 triage, which fully enriches KEV, federal, and EO 14028 critical software, roughly matches the narrow slice that matters. And NVD's universal enrichment was already failing (OIG) before AI discovery took off. Under this view, NIST's triage is a rational adaptation, not a capitulation.
- The rebuttal: unenriched CPE data breaks **automated matching** in scanners and SCA tools for the 80–85% of CVEs left out. The fact that only 0.23% are exploited does not tell a defender **which** unenriched CVE will be exploited next, and AI-found bugs skew toward RCE (50% vs. 26%).

- I found no published accuracy study of LLM-based CVSS/CWE/CPE assignment in production. GitHub's or other vendors' AI enrichment claims were not found in this pass.
- I found no primary EPSS or KEV time series confirming "flat" exploitability beyond FIRST's statement.

## Q5. Policy writing that connects AI discovery to NVD/CVE capacity specifically

### Takeaway
The direct link "AI discovery → NVD capacity crisis" is made mainly by the **Cloud Security Alliance** (a series of notes and a May 2026 whitepaper) and by trade press. NIST itself attributes the change only to a generic 263% surge, Mandiant/GTIG do not discuss NVD at all, and FIRST frames the constraint as CVD-wide human capacity. I found no Lawfare, think-tank, or academic piece in this pass that makes the specific connection, which suggests a gap the student's paper could fill.

### Cited Findings
- NIST's April 15, 2026 changes:
  - Prioritized enrichment for CISA KEV (target: within one business day), software used by the federal government, and EO 14028 critical software.
  - Everything else is labeled "Lowest Priority - not scheduled for immediate enrichment."
  - Backlogged CVEs published before Mar 1, 2026 moved to "Not Scheduled."
  - NIST will no longer routinely score CVEs a CNA has scored, and will reanalyze modified CVEs only if material.
  - Enrichment is available on request via email.
  - The notice does **not** mention AI — [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/04/nist-updates-nvd-operations-address-record-cve-growth)

It says NIST's criteria cover an estimated **15–20%** of expected volume, leaving 80–85% unenriched, with about 29,000 backlogged CVEs moved to "Not Scheduled." Recommendations: supplemental sources (VulnCheck NVD++, VulnDB, OSV, ENISA EUVD), a default "treat unknown as High," EPSS/KEV-based prioritization, NVD funding, CVE Foundation governance, and NVD–EUVD interoperability. It also cites a ~10% NIST budget cut in 2024 — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-whitepaper-nvd-infrastructure-crisis-ai-vulnerability-di/); [PDF](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CSA_whitepaper_NVD_infrastructure_crisis_AI_vulnerability_discovery_20260504-csa-styled.pdf) **[Advocacy org; many figures are second-hand. ### Inferences
- The causal chain "AI → CVE flood → NIST narrowed enrichment" is **asserted by CSA, not by NIST**. NIST's backlog began in Feb 2024 with a contractor funding lapse (OIG), well before Mythos or Glasswing (Apr–May 2026). To be accurate, the student should say AI discovery **worsens** a pre-existing structural capacity problem and makes NIST's April 2026 triage **permanent rather than temporary**. AI did not cause the problem.
- A policy framing supported by the evidence: if AI tools make discovery cheap (about $152/task at AIxCC; 90.6% true-positive rates in Glasswing), the scarce public good shifts from **finding** to **triage, enrichment, and remediation**. The same tools could be pointed at enrichment, but no one has yet shown measured accuracy.
